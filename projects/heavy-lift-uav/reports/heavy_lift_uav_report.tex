\section{Executive Summary \& Mission Requirements}

This report documents the engineering design and multi-physics validation of a heavy-lift multirotor Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV). The vehicle is engineered to carry a \textbf{10.0 kg payload} over a \textbf{30 km operational mission radius} with a \textbf{20-minute hover loiter} and a \textbf{20\% fuel reserve margin}.

The physical system consists of a \textbf{Single-Motor Hexacopter Topology} (6 arms at $60^\circ$ radial spacing, 1 motor + 1 ESC + 1 40-inch propeller per arm) powered by a \textbf{Gas-Electric Hybrid Generator} (3.6 kW continuous output driving a 48V DC bus).

\section{Aircraft Configuration \& Geometric Layout}

A trade study selected the single-motor hexacopter layout over coaxial octocopters (X8) to eliminate upper-to-lower rotor aerodynamic slipstream interference losses (which increase lower rotor hover power by 15--20\%).

For a 6-arm hexacopter with $60^\circ$ radial spacing, the tip-to-tip chord distance between adjacent arms equals arm length ($d = L$). With 40-inch propellers ($D_{\text{prop}} = 1.016\text{ m}$), setting $L = 1.120\text{ m}$ guarantees a tip-to-tip clearance of:
\begin{equation}
    d_{\text{clearance}} = L - D_{\text{prop}} = 1.120\text{ m} - 1.016\text{ m} = \mathbf{0.104\text{ m}} \quad (104\text{ mm}, 10.2\%\text{ clearance margin})
\end{equation}
This guarantees zero aerodynamic propeller overlap and reduces blade tip turbulence.

\end{itemize}

\section{Structural FEA Analysis \& Dual Load Cases}

The 1.12 m carbon fiber arm tubes (30 mm OD, 2 mm wall thickness, $I_{\text{area}} = 1.706 \times 10^{-8}\text{ m}^4$, $\text{UTS} = 800\text{ MPa}$, $E = 120\text{ GPa}$) were evaluated under two load cases in \href{https://github.com/Bishu-crypto/quadcopter-autonomy/blob/main/projects/heavy-lift-uav/design_calculations/structural_analysis.py}{\texttt{structural\_analysis.py}}.

\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \caption{Structural Load Case Evaluation \& Governing Case Selection}
    \begin{tabular}{lcc}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Structural Evaluation Parameter} & \textbf{Case 1: Symmetric 2.5G Limit} & \textbf{Case 2: Asymmetric Motor-Out (Governing)} \\
        \midrule
        Total Vertical Lift Force & $847.9\text{ N}$ & $508.8\text{ N}$ \\
        Effective Active Arm Count & 6 arms & 3 arms (moment balanced under recovery) \\
        Peak Force per Active Arm & $141.3\text{ N}$ & \textbf{169.6 N (+20.0\% higher load)} \\
        Root Bending Moment ($M = F \times L$) & $158.3\text{ N-m}$ & \textbf{189.9 N-m} \\
        Peak Bending Stress ($\sigma_{\max} = \frac{M c}{I}$) & $137.0\text{ MPa}$ & \textbf{164.4 MPa} \\
        Tip Deflection ($\delta = \frac{F L^3}{3 E I}$) & $11.60\text{ mm}$ & \textbf{13.92 mm} \\
        Safety Factor ($\text{SF} = \text{UTS} / \sigma_{\max}$) & $\text{SF} = 5.84$ & \textbf{SF = 4.87 (PASSED $\ge 1.5$)} \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The \textbf{Asymmetric Motor-Out Emergency Recovery Case GOVERNS} structural sizing because motor failure compensation concentrates higher force ($169.6\text{ N}$) on active arms adjacent to the failed rotor ($\sigma_{\max} = 164.4\text{ MPa}$, $\text{SF} = 4.87$).
